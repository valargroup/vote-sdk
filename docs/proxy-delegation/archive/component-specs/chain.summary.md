## key_decisions
- **The delegate key (DK) is Ed25519, verified with CometBFT crypto/ed25519 under ZIP-215 rules. Registration rejects non-canonical and small-order keys. Verifier attestation keys are also Ed25519.** — The identity proof post and the registration statement are verifiable in any language, and the identity research recommends Ed25519 identity keys. zcash_voting already ships ed25519-dalek, so Vizor needs no new crypto. A key that controls a whole pool can be held in an HSM, KMS or YubiKey. An Ed25519 key cannot be confused with Zcash spend authority. Verification is pure Go, deterministic, and real in every test build, unlike the RedPallas mock in untagged builds. A key_type field keeps room for agility. (rejected: RedPallas via the existing FFI: same group as Orchard spend keys, a mock verifier in untagged builds, no HSM path; its in-circuit future use can be covered by an optional separate Pallas key. secp256k1: delegates have no Cosmos accounts and Vizor has no secp256k1 key management.)
- **Routing happens in EndBlock, in the same step that moves a round from ACTIVE to TALLYING. For each (p,o) it stores agg = direct + sum of routed pools + Enc(0; rho), where rho = HashToScalar(domain || round || p || o || combined). It also stores the pre-routing base accumulator for audit.** — Pools are final when the transition block runs, because no reveal or route can land in it. PrepareProposal cannot write state, and partial decryption must see the final accumulators from the first TALLYING block. The re-randomizer prevents an identity C1 that the attacker can force. Without it, every DLEQ check fails and the round times out with empty results. The prototype reproduced this attack. (rejected: Lazy combination in PrepareProposal, ProcessProposal and SubmitTally: three places that must agree, and no state for auditing. Incremental routing at reveal or route time: more complex, and up to 50 additions per reveal. Re-randomizing with the block header hash: works, but makes auditing depend on header data.)
- **A MsgRevealShare with proposal_id 0 is accepted only if the round has proxy delegation enabled and 1 <= decision < NextDelegateIndex. Delegate status is not checked at reveal time; status only controls whether the delegate can route.** — The delegate index is private inside ZKP4, so the chain cannot check it when the DC is created. If reveals were rejected once a delegate was suspended, honest delegators' weight would be stranded without warning. A pool that cannot route simply abstains, which is what product decision 2 specifies. The registry is append-only, so 'index exists' is monotone and deterministic. (rejected: Require registration before round creation: blocks mid-round registrations and cannot be enforced when the DC is created. Require ACTIVE status at reveal: weight is lost silently, and the outcome is the same as abstaining anyway.)
- **ZKP4 exposes a public delegate_index_bound, and the chain requires 1 <= bound < NextDelegateIndex when the DC is submitted.** — This turns 'delegated to an index that does not exist' from weight burned silently at reveal time into a rejected transaction that spends nothing. It leaks almost nothing, because wallets use the current NextDelegateIndex, and it costs only a small range check in the circuit. (rejected: Make the delegate index public in ZKP4 (B1-style): links the delegate set to the anonymous transaction. Check only at reveal time: keeps as a fallback if the circuit cannot afford the range check.)
- **Add a mixed action batch MsgVoteActionBatch (tag 0x09) and a composite MsgDelegateAndVoteActionBatch (tag 0x0B). Actions are a oneof of proxy_delegation or cast. DCs come first (1 to 10 of them), at most 50 actions in total, signed under one batch digest and chained through single-leaf roots.** — This reuses the proven batch and composite pattern, including the atomic all-checks-before-writes handler. The ordering rule follows from the circuit's authority == MAX requirement. Capping at 50 actions keeps the worst-case transaction the same size as today's composite, under the REST and CometBFT 1 MB limits. The oneof leaves room for future cast variants, such as private-vote v2. (rejected: Extend MsgCastVoteBatch or MsgDelegateAndCastVoteBatch in place: breaks old binaries and the dormant policy. Separate one-DC transactions: lose atomicity and leak timing. A 60-action limit: gets close to the 1 MB REST and CometBFT caps.)
- **Proxy delegation is enabled per round by snapshotting the coordinator-set ProxyDelegationParams.enable_for_new_rounds into VoteRound.proxy_delegation when the round is created, along with a stored cutoff_time.** — A new field on MsgCreateVotingSession would break the dormant-merge policy. Old binaries reject unknown fields inside the coordinator Any payload at decode time, while a dormant new binary would fail with a different code, so LastResultsHash would diverge. Wallets can verify the snapshot with an IAVL proof, and it fails safe if misreported. (rejected: A field on MsgCreateVotingSession, added only in the activation commit: possible but awkward. Binding the flag into the round ID: breaks every client's round-ID handling. A chain-wide flag with no per-round snapshot: makes rounds mutable.)
- **DC cutoff = vote_end_time - clamp((vote_end_time - created_at_time)/10, 60 s, 3600 s), stored in the round. Routes and reveals stay open until vote_end_time.** — This leaves time for 16 helper reveals per DC and matches the submission-server ZIP's last-moment buffer. There is no override, so delegators need no window to react to routes. Routes are single signatures that do not depend on helpers. (rejected: Accept DCs until vote_end_time: risks partial pools landing in single-share last-moment mode. A 40%/6 h window: blocks proxy delegation for too long. A route deadline earlier than vote_end_time: no benefit without an override.)
- **Registry design: a global, append-only index (starting at 1). Registration is a fee-less custom-wire message carrying a threshold Ed25519 attestation over a digest bound to the chain ID, a DK proof-of-possession, and an opaque salted identity_commitment. Keys can be rotated with the old key (effective immediately) or recovered with an attestation (after a 48 h delay). Retirement is self-signed and terminal. Suspension is a coordinator action. Old keys can never be registered again.** — The chain can authenticate routes without X content on chain, which helps with X and GDPR deletion duties. Spam is gated in CheckTx by the attestation. Indices stay stable across key changes, so existing pools keep working. The cooling-off period covers X account takeover. (rejected: Standard Cosmos transactions: delegates have no accounts and bank sends are disabled. Storing X handles or IDs in plaintext: deletion duty and handle recycling. Deduplicating attestations by signature bytes: the ICNS bug.)
- **New KV prefixes 0x1A to 0x1F (record, key index, route set, params, next index, routing base). Historically used 0x09 and 0x0D are avoided. Pools reuse TallyKey(round,0,d) and ShareCountKey(round,0,d).** — Every fact a delegator verifies is a single IAVL point lookup that Vizor's existing light client can prove. Existing genesis paths for tally accumulators and share counts already accept proposal 0. (rejected: Reuse 0x09 or 0x0D: they may still hold old data. Per-(round,d,p) route keys: 50 proofs instead of one per delegate.)
- **Add a tree-capacity guard inside AppendCommitment (ErrCommitmentTreeFull), plus an early check in ante, and correct MaxTreePosition to 2^24-1.** — A failed DeliverTx rolls back the whole transaction, so every handler, old and new, becomes safe. EndBlock can then never hit the chain-halting full-tree path. (rejected: Only pre-checks in the handlers: easy to miss in a future handler.)

## baseline_challenges
- HIGH (round-killing griefing, not fatal to B2): naive EndBlock routing can store an accumulator whose C1 is the identity point. An attacker who controls all randomness in two pools, or in one pool plus their own direct votes on an unused option, picks r values so the sum is zero. VerifyPartialDecryptDLEQ rejects identity C1 (crypto/elgamal/dleq.go:95-96). SubmitPartialDecryption needs a valid DLEQ for every non-empty accumulator (msg_server_tally_decrypt.go:249-279), so no partial decryption is accepted and the round times out with EMPTY results. Prototype-confirmed in proto-chain/route_test.go. FIX: add a deterministic Enc(0; rho) to every routed bucket, with rho = HashToScalar('svote-route-rerand-v1' || round || p || o || combined). This is cheap (about 1 ms per bucket), keeps the plaintext unchanged and stays auditable.
- MEDIUM: the baseline hides the delegate index inside the DC but gives the chain no way to catch an invalid index before reveal. A client bug or a stale registry view would burn weight silently. FIX: ZKP4 exposes a public delegate_index_bound, proving 1 <= d <= bound, and the chain requires bound < NextDelegateIndex when the DC is submitted. If the circuit cannot afford it, the fallback is rejection at reveal time with a permanent error.
- MEDIUM: 'decision must be a registered delegate index valid for this round' must not depend on delegate status. Suspending a delegate after delegators' DCs would otherwise strand their weight silently, and the chain cannot see d when the DC is created. FIX: accept a reveal if the index exists (1 <= d < NextDelegateIndex). Status only controls routing, so an inactive delegate's pool abstains.
- MEDIUM (process): a per-round enable field on MsgCreateVotingSession breaks the dormant-merge policy. Old binaries reject unknown critical fields inside the coordinator Any at decode time. A dormant binary would fail later with a different ABCI code, and Code is hashed into LastResultsHash (cometbft types/results.go:45-54). FIX: snapshot a coordinator-set ProxyDelegationParams.enable_for_new_rounds into VoteRound.proxy_delegation when the round is created. All new type registration stays behind the dormant flag.
- LOW-MEDIUM: one composite with ZKP1, 10 ZKP4 and 50 ZKP2 proofs is about 690 KB raw, or about 920 KB as base64 JSON. That is close to the 1,000,000-byte REST listener and CometBFT RPC caps. FIX: cap each message at 50 actions in total (the same envelope as today's composite). Wallets put any remaining casts in a follow-up 0x06 batch anchored to the new final VAN.
- LOW (inform product): the number of delegations each delegate receives is public by construction. Each reveal transaction carries vote_decision = d in the clear, so ShareCount(round,0,d)/16 is roughly the number of DCs. Product decision 4 hides totals but not counts, so this is a count oracle (Clark et al.). Totals can also be inferred by differencing whenever a delegate is the only contributor to a bucket. Mitigations, such as dummy DCs, are outside the chain's scope.
- LOW: ProposalTally(proposal_id=0) would return an unpaginated map of every pool (query_server.go:118-135). FIX: reject proposal 0 there and serve pools from a paginated DelegatePools query.
- LOW: MaxTreePosition says 2^32-1 but the tree has depth 24 (keys.go:76-79), and ErrCommitmentTreeFull is registered but never used. FIX: correct the constant and add the guard inside AppendCommitment.
- NAMING: 'delegation commitment' and 'ZKP4 delegation-commitment proof' collide with ZKP1 'delegation'. Use proxy_delegation_commitment, ProxyDelegationAction, VerifyProxyDelegation and sv_verify_proxy_delegation_proof in code. Keep 'delegate' (noun) for the registered person.
- COMPATIBILITY: if private-vote v2 lands, the chain must keep the v1 scalar ZKP3 verifier and DOMAIN_VC for DCs, and restrict tag 0x04 to proposal_id 0 in v2 rounds. ZKP4 must stay a sibling of the v1 scalar cast relation, not of compact v2 ZKP2. Routing is unaffected.
- NOTE: routing cost is one-off but grows with the number of routes. On an M3 Ultra, 1,000 delegates x 50 routes took about 0.5 s and 10,000 x 50 about 1.4 s in the prototype. This is acceptable, but add a CI benchmark and monitoring; if the registry grows beyond about 10k active delegates, switch to incremental routing.

## open_questions_for_owner
- DC cutoff: is vote_end_time - clamp(10% of the round, 60 s, 1 h) acceptable? Recommendation: yes. It matches the submission-server last-moment buffer and gives DC shares time to land, while late arrivals can still vote directly.
- When a delegate is suspended (impersonation or key compromise), should routes it already made be voided, turning them into abstentions? Recommendation: no. Routes are final, and suspension only blocks future routes. Voiding would give the coordinator a lever to erase a delegate's votes until vote_end_time.
- Should delegates have an explicit public 'abstain' route (decision 0xFFFFFFFF, recorded but not counted) so delegators can tell 'chose to abstain' from 'did not vote'? Recommendation: yes. It costs almost nothing on chain and improves accountability.
- How long should key recovery without the old key take (cooling-off)? Recommendation: 48 hours. The old key can cancel during that window, and the coordinator can suspend.
- Should there be a minimum weight per proxy delegation beyond 1 ballot (0.125 ZEC)? Recommendation: no. Delegates do O(1) work in B2, and the leaf and helper amplification is already bounded below today's votes.
- Should per-delegate pool share counts (about 16 x the number of delegators) be exposed in a query, given they are derivable from public reveal transactions anyway? Recommendation: yes, expose them honestly, but Vizor should not present them as a leaderboard.
- Verifier set at launch: 1-of-1 Valar, or t-of-n? Recommendation: launch 1-of-1 with the threshold machinery already on chain, and add a second independent verifier before featured delegates control large pools.
- Should proxy delegation be allowed for a VAN that has already voted on some proposals? This would need per-proposal pools and a circuit change. Recommendation: no for v1. Vizor tells users to delegate before casting any vote.

## risks
- If the routing re-randomization is left out or implemented wrongly, an attacker can make every partial decryption fail and the whole round times out with empty results. This needs a dedicated regression test that uses the identity-attack vector.
- Dormant-gating mistakes, such as registering a new type or decoding a new field while dormant, change ABCI result codes. Because Code is hashed into LastResultsHash, mixed versions would halt the chain. Mitigate by restoring the removed dormancy-equivalence tests.
- A delegate key compromise is irreversible within a round: the attacker can route all remaining proposals once. Suspension only stops future routes. Ed25519 HSM custody and old-key rotation reduce the risk.
- The X-verifier is a single point of trust and of censorship for new registrations, and X API access may be withdrawn, as happened to ICNS and the Gitcoin X stamp. Non-X providers (GitHub, domain) need to ship early.
- Privacy leaks inherent to B2: the number of delegations per delegate is public (count oracle), and a delegate's pool size can be inferred by differencing when it is the only contributor to an option. A colluding EA threshold can decrypt pools, as it can decrypt individual shares today.
- Helper capacity near the end of a round: DC shares compete with vote shares under the 256-reveals-per-block cap. Shares that miss vote_end_time partially strand pooled weight, as with late votes today.
- EndBlock routing time grows with the number of routes (about 1.4 s for 10k delegates x 50 proposals on an M3 Ultra). Slower validator hardware could stretch the transition block.
- Delegators who voted on anything before proxy-delegating cannot proxy-delegate that VAN, because the circuit requires full authority. Without clear wallet UX this becomes a usability trap.
- REST and CometBFT 1 MB limits for large composite transactions, if wallets ignore the 50-action cap or ZKP4 proofs turn out larger than ZKP2's.
- Private-vote v2 rollout: forgetting to keep the scalar ZKP3 for proposal 0, or to restrict tag 0x04 to proposal 0 in v2 rounds, would break DC reveals or weaken domain separation.
- Light-client privacy: targeted abci_query calls for a specific delegate's routes or for share nullifiers reveal interest to the RPC operator unless wallets download whole pages or use Tor.
- A wallet that trusts a REST server for the handle-to-index mapping can be steered to an attacker's delegate index. Wallets must check the signed snapshot against the IAVL-proven record.
